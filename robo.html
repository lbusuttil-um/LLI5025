The Robo-Marker

A school wants to save teachers time by training a model to mark short science answers. It has been given eight answers that a teacher has already marked. As with the Glorps, nobody tells it what a good answer looks like. It only sees the answers and the marks.

What the Robo-Marker learned

In the training set, every answer with the mark was long and used the word "faster". Every answer without the mark was short and did not. Either of those is far easier to detect than whether the pupil understood that light travels faster than sound, so that is what the model leans on.

The result: it rewards a long, confident, wrong answer (B) and refuses short correct ones (C, D, E). It is the Glorp problem again, only now the "colour" is word count and vocabulary.

Four things to take away

  • Shortcut learning happens with text too. Length, vocabulary and confident phrasing are easy signals that often go with good answers but are not the same thing.
  • Out of distribution. Answer D is correct, in Maltese. The model has never seen Maltese, so it has nothing to go on. Systems trained mostly on English often do worse with Maltese, and with pupils who switch between the two.
  • Who pays for the shortcut? Answer E shows real understanding with weak spelling. A shortcut-driven marker would consistently penalise pupils with dyslexia, younger writers and those writing in a second language.
  • Who decides the label? Teachers often split on F, which describes what happens without explaining why. A model trained on one teacher's marks learns that teacher's judgement and presents it as fact.

This has happened

Automated essay scoring systems have been criticised for rewarding length and unusual vocabulary. In 2014, writing researcher Les Perelman showed that essays of grammatical nonsense, produced by a program he built for the purpose, could score highly on automated scorers. Today's AI marking and feedback tools are far more capable, but the question is the same: what is the tool actually responding to?

Talk about it

  • How would you change the eight training answers so the Robo-Marker could not take this shortcut?
  • Would you trust an AI tool to give pupils feedback on short answers? What would you check first?
  • AI detectors claim to spot AI-written work. Based on today, what might they really be responding to?
