Activity 4 · hands on

Train it, then break it

Every group trains a cat or dog classifier, but no two groups get the same training pictures. Your job is to find out what your model really learned, then hunt the web for pictures that fool it. The best failure is not the funniest one. It is the one you predicted and can explain.

1

Train your model

  1. Download your group's picture set from Moodle (your set number is your group number) and unzip it. Inside are two folders: Cats and Dogs. On an iPad, tap the zip in the Files app to unzip it.
    Picture sets on Moodle
  2. Open Teachable Machine and start an Image project with the Standard image model.
  3. Rename Class 1 to Cat and Class 2 to Dog.
  4. In the Cat class, choose Upload and select every picture in the Cats folder. Do the same for Dogs. On a laptop you can drag the whole set of files in at once.
  5. Press Train Model and keep the tab open until it finishes. On a tablet this takes a little longer.

Training happens in your browser. Your pictures are not uploaded anywhere unless you choose to save the project to Google Drive.

2

Audit your training data

Before you test anything, look through your own pictures as a group. A model can only learn from what it was shown, so its blind spots are in this list. Jot down what you notice.

  • How many?Are there about as many cats as dogs, or is one class much bigger?
  • Which breeds and colours?What kinds of cat and dog are there? Which are missing?
  • How old?Kittens and puppies, adults, or a mix?
  • Where?Indoors, outdoors, grass, sofas, plain backgrounds? Is one class always in the same kind of place?
  • How are they shown?Close-up faces, whole bodies, odd angles, with people?
  • Anything odd?Duplicates, blurry photos, a picture in the wrong folder?
3

Predict, then hunt

Choose a gap from your audit and write down which picture you think will fool the model, and why, before you search. Then find a picture on the web, save it, and test it: in the Preview panel, switch the input from Webcam to File and upload it.

1

Something missing

A breed, colour or age that is not in your set. A Siamese, a Sphynx, a Pomeranian, a kitten.

2

The wrong place

If your cats were mostly indoors, try a cat in a garden. Does the background matter more than the animal?

3

A hard view

From behind, from above, curled up asleep, half hidden under a blanket.

4

The impostor

A fox, a lion, a cartoon cat, a toy dog. The model has no way to say "neither". How sure is its wrong answer?

Keep SafeSearch on. Pictures from the web are fine for testing here, but don't republish them.

4

Capture the failure

Take a screenshot that shows both the picture and the confidence bars in the Preview panel.

WindowsWindows + Shift + S, then drag around the Preview panel
MacCmd + Shift + 4, then drag around the Preview panel
iPadPress the top button and volume up together
ChromebookCtrl + Shift + Show windows

6

Cross-test

Send your best picture to a neighbouring group and ask them to run it through their model. If it fools yours but not theirs, the difference has to be in the training data, because everything else was the same.

Finished early? Try a model for your own subject

Build a second model with the webcam or microphone. Pick a card, collect your own examples, and try to break it in the same way.
