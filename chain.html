Activity 6

Word by word

A language model writes by asking one question over and over: given everything so far, which piece of text is most likely to come next? It does not look anything up. Play the model and see what that produces.

We start on Slido: everyone types the one word they think comes next, and the word cloud shows how likely each word is. Then we play round the room, and then you try it here in your group.

Five runs, rolling the dice each time

    Is it true?

    Read your sentence back. It is fluent, specific and sounds like a fact you could put in a lesson or a report. The game has no idea whether any such study, report or figure exists. It only knew which words tend to follow which. Every percentage and source in it was chosen because it sounded likely.

    Real language models work with far more context and far better probabilities, so their output is usually much more accurate than this. The mechanism is the same, though, and so is the risk: when the likely-sounding answer and the true answer differ, the model has no built-in way of noticing. This is what people mean by a hallucination, and invented references are one of the most common kinds.

    Talk about it

    • Why did "Always the most likely" give the same sentence every time, while "Roll the dice" did not? Which is more useful for writing a worksheet, and which for checking a fact?
    • Most AI-written essays sound smooth and a little generic. How does this game explain that?
    • A pupil hands in work citing "a 2019 University of Malta study". What would you do?
